You are absolutely right. "Syntax Studio" is what a 22-year-old CS grad names their first LLC. It has no soul.

Since you are a **musician** (structure, rhythm, interplay), a **philosopher** (inquiry, truth), and working with a **Korean partner** (cross-cultural, specific aesthetics), your name should come from *those* deep wells.

Here are four specific directions that are not generic, with the "Why" based on your background.

### 1. The "Texture & Flow" Angle (Korean Concept)
**Name:** **Kyeol Studio** (or **Gyeol**)
*   **The Origin:** The Korean word **결 (Gyeol)**.
*   **The Meaning:** It is untranslatable but perfect for you. It means "texture" or "grain" (like wood grain), but also "wave," "breathing," and "flow."
*   **Why it fits:**
    *   **Linguistics:** You are analyzing the "texture" of language (Discourse Analysis).
    *   **Code:** You are organizing data into a coherent "grain."
    *   **Music:** It implies a natural rhythm or flow.
    *   **The Pitch:** "We don't just build apps; we find the *Gyeol*—the natural grain—of your research and build around it."

### 2. The "Philosophy of Making" Angle (Classics)
**Name:** **Techne Logic** (or just **Techne**)
*   **The Origin:** The Ancient Greek **Technē (τέχνη)**.
*   **The Meaning:** It is often translated as "craft" or "art," but for Aristotle, it meant the **rational method involved in producing an object.** It is the root of "Technology," but it implies *craftsmanship* and *knowledge*, not just machines.
*   **Why it fits:**
    *   **Philosophy:** It distinguishes you from "Labor" (mindless work). You are doing *Techne*—thoughtful creation.
    *   **Education:** It implies teaching a skill or craft.
    *   **The Pitch:** "Restoring the 'Techne'—the craft and reasoning—to Educational Technology."


        
This is the right strategic move. In education, **trust** is the currency. A "Consultancy" feels safer to a University Department Chair than a "Freelancer."

Here is your **Website Blueprint** for the "Pedagogical Engineer" Studio.

### 1. The Vibe (Visual Identity)
You need to walk a fine line.
*   **Too "Academic":** You look like a university department page (boring, dusty, static).
*   **Too "Tech Startup":** You look like a crypto scam (dark mode, neon gradients, buzzwords).
*   **The Goal:** **"Modern Laboratory."** Clean, white space, precise typography, structured. Think of the website for *Linear* or *Vercel*, but with academic serif fonts (like *Georgia* or *Merriweather*) for headings.

**The Palette:**
*   **Deep Navy or Slate Blue:** Signals "Institutional Trust" (Education).
*   **Off-White/Cream:** Signals "Paper/Reading" (Research).
*   **Accent Color:** A sharp "Teal" or "Burnt Orange" for buttons (Tech/Action).

### 2. The Sitemap (Keep it Simple)
Don't overbuild. You need 4 pages to start.

1.  **Home** (The Hook)
2.  **Services** (The Offer)
3.  **Lab / Case Studies** (The Proof)
4.  **About** (The Credentials)

---

### 3. Page-by-Page Content Strategy

#### A. The Home Page
**Hero Section:**
*   **Headline:** "We Build Evidence-Based Technology for Language Education."
*   **Sub-headline:** "Bridging the gap between Applied Linguistics research and production-grade software. Custom tools for universities, publishers, and research labs."
*   **Primary CTA:** "View Our Work"
*   **Secondary CTA:** "Book a Consultation"

**Social Proof Bar:**
*   Instead of client logos (which you don't have yet), use **Institutional Affiliations**:
    *   *Bad:* "As seen on..."
    *   *Good:* "Our team brings experience from:" [Logos for Fulbright, USF, FSU, TESOL]

#### B. The Services Page (The "Menu")
Divide this into two clear tracks so visitors self-select immediately.

**Track 1: For Educators & Departments**
*   **Headline:** "Curriculum Digitization"
*   **Copy:** "Stop using generic tools for specific courses. We build custom platforms that match your exact syllabus."
*   **Bullet points:**
    *   Custom Vocabulary & Grammar Engines.
    *   AI-powered Practice Labs (Safe & Guardrailed).
    *   LMS Integration (Canvas/Blackboard).

**Track 2: For Researchers & PIs**
*   **Headline:** "Research Instrumentation"
*   **Copy:** "Modernize your methodology. We build the software that powers your study."
*   **Bullet points:**
    *   Automated Qualitative Coding Pipelines.
    *   Data Cleaning & Visualization.
    *   Broader Impacts Educational Tools (Grant-ready).

#### C. The "Lab" (Portfolio) Page
Rename "Portfolio" to **"Lab"** or **"Work"**. It sounds more scientific.
*   **Project 1: TapedChinese** -> *Case Study: "Reducing Lesson Planning Time via LLM Automation."* (Focus on the *metric*).
*   **Project 2: Fulbright Planner** -> *Case Study: "Standardizing Volunteer Tutoring Across 50+ Sites."*
*   **Project 3: The Conference App** (The one you are building now) -> *Case Study: "Real-time Data Visualization for Academic Conferences."*

#### D. The About Page (Crucial for Consultants)
This is where you sell **You**.
*   **The Narrative:** "We aren't just developers. We are Applied Linguists who code."
*   **Your Bio:** Highlight the "Hybrid" nature. "Larry has published research on genre analysis AND shipped production-grade apps."
*   **Your Partner:** Highlight her academic rank. "Dr. [Name] ensures every tool is grounded in current pedagogical theory."

---

### 4. The "Consultant" Call-to-Action
Most consultancy sites have a generic "Contact Us" form. **Don't do that.** It invites spam and low-quality leads.

Create a **"Project Inquiry"** form with specific dropdowns:
*   *"I am a..."* [Department Head, Grant PI, Textbook Publisher]
*   *"I need help with..."* [Digitizing a Curriculum, Automating Research Data, AI Strategy Workshop]

**The "Magic" Question:**
Add a field: *"What is the biggest friction point in your current workflow?"*
*   This gives you the talking points for your first sales call before you even pick up the phone.

### 5. Launch Plan (The "Minimum Viable Website")

Since you want to move fast:

**Week 1:**
*   Buy the domain.
*   Put up a **"One-Pager"**.
    *   Top: Hero + Value Prop.
    *   Middle: 3-Card "Services" section.
    *   Bottom: "About" section + Contact Form.
*   *Why?* You need a URL to put on the business cards for the conference.

**Week 2-4 (Before the Conference):**
*   Flesh out the **"Lab"** section with the interactive tool you are building for your girlfriend.
*   Write **one** blog post/article: *"Why Generic AI Tools Fail in the Language Classroom"* (This establishes your philosophy).

**The Tech Stack:**
*   Since you are a dev, build it in **SvelteKit** (since you know it) or **Astro** (fast, content-focused).
*   Host on **Vercel** (Free tier is fine for now).
*   Use a clean UI library like **Tailwind UI** or **Shadcn** so you don't waste time designing buttons.
